# Why I am starting a dev blog

I never thought I'd be a blog person. I thought blogs were only written by people with big egos or great wisdom. I don't think I have either, but there may be a third reason.

Recently I moved companies (I'm a software engineer if that wasn't clear). Overall it's been a pleasant experience, however it came with a couple of surprises.

I expected that the difficulty would come from learning new codebases and technologies. In reality, **that part was easy**. What was _actually_ challenging was everything surrounding it — the processes. Learning how my new team worked, their culture, motivations, workflows, and engineering practices.

At my previous job, I assumed that everything I learned about engineering was **universal**. That every company did PR reviews, regression testing, etc. the same way. It's become clear to me that this is not the case. And in the past few months I've been trying to decipher how much of that difference is due to _different company requirements_, and how much is due to _orgs being at different maturities_ in their tech journeys.

The answer, it turns out, is a mix of both. Some differences were genuinely due to the nature of the work — I moved from stable high-volume systems to smaller, more business-adaptable features. Others came down to **org maturity**: practices my old team had learned the hard way, my new team simply hadn't needed yet. And vice versa.

It's forced me to reflect on many aspects of the software development lifecycle. And in the interest of documenting some learnings (and perhaps sharing with others) I will be writing those reflections in this blog. So if you're interested in my thoughts on the _exhilarating_ topics of automation testing, pipelines, code quality, etc. — there is more to come!
